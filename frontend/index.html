THE CONTEXT

When seeing is
no longer believing.

A deepfake is synthetic video, audio, or imagery made with AI to convincingly replace or imitate a person’s face, voice, or actions.

That can be used for harmless creativity, but it can also power misinformation, identity theft, financial scams, harassment, and political manipulation. The most important defense is informed attention.

SPOT THE SIGNS

Pause.
Look closer.
Verify.

One clue alone cannot prove a deepfake. Look for a pattern, then check the source.

  • 01

    Movement feels unnatural

    Watch for odd blinking, stiff expressions, or lip movements that do not match the audio.

  • 02

    Details do not hold together

    Look around the face and hair for blur, distorted edges, and inconsistent lighting or skin tone.

  • 03

    The source is uncertain

    Be cautious with dramatic claims from accounts or sites you cannot verify.

PROTECT YOURSELF

Verify before
you believe.

Before you share, take a minute to check.

  1. 01
    Check the source

    Look for an official account or a credible original publication.

  2. 02
    Cross-check the claim

    Compare it with trusted reporting or other independent sources.

  3. 03
    Confirm unusual requests

    If someone asks for money or personal details, contact them through a separate channel.

  4. 04
    Limit what you share

    Use privacy settings and be thoughtful about public photos, videos, and voice clips.
